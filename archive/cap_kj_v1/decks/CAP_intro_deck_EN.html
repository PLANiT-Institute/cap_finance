01 · Why now
Where CAP comes from

We have measured the cost of transition for years. What has been missing is the cost of waiting.

What we have built

Years of industrial-decarbonisation research: abatement-cost analysis, technology-transition assessment, a dedicated steel-sector study, and asset-level financial-risk models.

What we learned

Companies already know the cost is enormous. Another, larger estimate changes no decision — by itself, the cost of transition is an argument for hesitation.

What follows

The number that changes decisions is the price of delay — what is lost when a decision slips past the investment window. CAP measures exactly that.

15–20 yrs
One relining cycle — one decision window. The blast-furnace windows of the early 2030s are the last before 2050. Miss one and there is no low-cost path back: either a generation of emissions is locked in, or policy forces early closure and the remaining asset value is written off.

04 · Expected outputs
Output 1 · The core exhibit

The frontier, the gap — and what five years of delay does to both

Investors will recognise this picture: a Markowitz frontier for one company's transition plans. On the curve, cost and risk trade off — paying for PPAs, CCfDs and flexible technology raises expected cost and cuts the tail. A plan that looks inexpensive is often simply unhedged — the risk remains, merely unpriced.

The dot is the announced plan; the gap reads in two directions — spending too little to align (cost gap), or spending unprotected against energy-price extremes (risk gap). That is the assessment: is this firm under-investing, or under-hedging?

Delay is not drift — it converts planned reinvestment into forced early closure: policy still binds, but compliance arrives as write-offs of remaining asset value instead of conversions.

Wedge shows both scenarios at once — the distance between the two frontiers is metric ④: how much depends on policy ambition.

05 · Why it matters
One diagnosis, three readers

A common language for the efficiency of net-zero capital

For the company  The gap prices, in won, what each board-approvable lever would recover — the rebuild window, the PPA, the CCfD, the fixed-price EPC.

For the investor  Transition risk becomes measured exposure, and the delay case turns “engage now” into arithmetic — with a time series to hold plans accountable.

For the policymaker  Gross-vs-net accounting shows which instrument reduces which firm's risk, and by how much.

Not a MACCadds capital timing, deliverability and residual risk to the cost curve
Not a scoreno composite index, no rankings — dimensions stay visible
Not an invented premiumexposures now; pricing waits for market evidence
The thesis: capital should flow to the most efficient (cost, risk) combination available — while the windows are open.

06 · Method
Method in one slide

How it works, in three steps

1
Scenarios set the rules, not the answers. They provide only the sector's carbon budget and prices; an optimisation over the firm's actual facilities decides which plant converts, to what, and when — and charges off-window conversion as early closure, writing off remaining asset value.
2
Every plan is stress-tested against 5,000 joint price paths for power, hydrogen and construction costs, calibrated to more than ten years of market data.
3
The firm is compared with itself. The best achievable trade-offs form the frontier; the announced plan's distance from it is the gap; re-running the optimisation with missed windows prices the delay.
